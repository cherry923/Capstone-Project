                                  Adil Hussain1, Qing-Chang Lu1, Sanam Shahla Rizvi2, Shixin Wang1 & Se Jin Kwon3
                                  The growing number of electric vehicles (EVs) on the road poses great challenges to the power supply
                                  and causes outages. Most existing research works focus on individual or aggregated charging station
                                  data at the city level. However, charging behaviors at different city locations might demonstrate
                                  different patterns and characteristics. This study proposed a Context-Aware Temporal Transformer
                                  (CAT-Former) model using Temporal and Contextual features for short-term EV charging demand
                                  forecasting of one hour and one day ahead using the public EV data from Boulder City, Colorado. The
                                  temporal and contextual features are important features, which help the model to understand the
                                  charging patterns of different periods over different locations. The charging data with different trends
                                  is crucial to train and test the proposed model performance. Therefore, this study chose the three
                                  locations with the highest number of sessions from the data. The performance of the proposed model,
                                  as well as the baseline models, including LSTM, BiLSTM, and hybrid models such as CNN-LSTM and
                                  CNN-BiLSTM, is assessed and compared using Mean Square Error (MSE) and Mean Absolute Error
                                  (MAE) on three locations. The proposed model is compared to the Simple and Hybrid Transformer
                                  models utilizing the LSTM-based Encoder-Decoder. The proposed model performed better than the
                                  baseline models for one hour and one day ahead of forecasting for the selected locations by achieving
                                  the lowest MSE and MAE values. The results show that the proposed CAT-Former model using
                                  temporal and contextual features can effectively forecast the charging demand using charging data
                                  from different locations for short-term periods, including one-hour and one-day ahead predictions.

                                  charging demand, which is influenced by various factors. To improve the industrial and social economy while
                                  lowering emissions of greenhouse gases, it is becoming increasingly important to operate transportation and

                                  system are greatly impacted by load predictions11. The accuracy of EV charging forecasts is critical to utilities’

                                  data-driven and model-based forecasts12. In contrast to residential load power and wind power predictions, which
                                  are mostly affected by the surrounding environment and resident lifestyles, the first prediction form focuses on
                                  EV users’ charging behavior. Statistical and machine learning algorithms are commonly used for load forecasting
                                  methodologies covering EV charging demand13. Autoregressive (AR), Moving Average (MA), Autoregressive
                                  Moving Average (ARMA), and Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) are statistical time series
                                  forecasting methods that are frequently employed for short-term load forecasting. These methods are based on
                                  the assumption that forecasting data exhibits a linear correlation with historical load data. Consequently, they
                                  are unable to accurately represent the nonlinear characteristics of the historical data that has been observed.
                                  Deep neural networks and other machine learning methodologies are more effective than statistical models
                                  in managing complex and non-linear data characteristics. In the process of load forecasting, the Support
                                  Vector Machine (SVM) and Support Vector Regression (SVR) models are used14. To forecast EV charging
                                  demand, advanced deep learning methodologies, such as Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Convolutional
                                  Neural Networks (CNN), and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), have been implemented, including their
                                  ensembles. Several researchers have recently concentrated on developing load forecasting methods based on
                                  deep learning15,16.

                                  demand forecasts; however, different charging locations in a city have different charging demands and grid loads.
                                  The accuracy and performance of the model using different CS data need to be validated. The main contribution
                                  of this work is the development of a Context-Aware Temporal Transformer (CAT-Former) model based on
                                  Contextual and Temporal Features, which is implemented using a public dataset of Boulder City. Furthermore,
                                  the proposed model is evaluated based on different charging locations within the city to analyze the performance
                                  of different charging trends. The data used for the model training and testing are collected from multiple
                                  locations selected, based on the highest number of charging sessions and high charging demand in the city. The
                                  main contribution of this study is the proposed CAT-Former model, which utilizes the temporal and contextual
                                  features and provides short-term predictions with better performance than the LSTM and Transformer models.

                                  • To address the temporal and contextual features and propose a Context-Aware Temporal Transformer
                                    (CAT-Former) model for short-term EV charging demand forecasting.
                                  • As the charging trends differ between locations, we use three locations with the highest charging demand to
                                    evaluate the charging demand forecast performance for multiple locations.
                                  • To evaluate the performance of the proposed CAT-Former model with traditional deep learning-based indi-
                                    vidual and hybrid models, along with the Transformer and Hybrid Transformer models.

                                  Literature review
                                  To study the effects of EV charging on the electrical grid, it is necessary to predict the demand for EV charging.
                                  Accurate EV charging predictions are essential for utility decision-making and growth. In addition to offering
                                  solutions for the planning of CS development and improving the accuracy of optimal dispatching predictions.
                                  As well as providing manufacturers with significant incentives for adopting EVs. Researchers have recently
                                  developed several methods to enhance the effectiveness of various load forecasting techniques by applying
                                  traditional methods or Artificial Intelligence (AI). However, traditional forecasting methods cannot fully utilize
                                  the external factors influencing the power consumption of the CS14. Thus, these traditional methods fall short in
                                  effectiveness15. The rapid advancement of AI, machine learning (ML), and deep learning has enabled intelligent
                                  algorithms to significantly contribute to the success of big data technologies across various applications16. The
                                  rapid advancement of AI technology has positively impacted power grids. Various algorithms, including Support
                                  Vector Machines (SVMs), Random Forests (RF), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), have been utilized to
                                  address the diverse challenges faced by the power grid. These techniques have been thoroughly examined in the
                                  context of power systems.

                                  Deep learning
                                  There is a high demand for deep learning-based methods to uncover the latent features of EV charges and
                                  improve the accuracy of the forecasting model. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) represent a significant
                                  advancement in deep learning techniques, particularly in the context of time series forecasting. The two widely
                                  recognized types of RNNs, specifically the Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs)17 and the Long Short-Term Memory
                                  (LSTM)18, have been explored in terms of their application with time series forecasting. Li et al.19 also examined
                                  the effectiveness of CNN, another deep-learning approach. Zhu et al.11 performed a comparative analysis of
                                  several well-known deep-learning methodologies. Numerical analyses at various time intervals indicate
                                  that deep learning approaches may accurately forecast short-term power consumption. Moreover, the study
                                  demonstrated that LSTM surpasses other deep learning algorithms in reducing prediction errors. LSTM models,
                                  both univariate and multivariate, have shown higher performance than traditional machine learning models.
                                  When comparing different types of LSTM models, multivariate LSTM models, which consider factors such as
                                  wind speed, temperature, and humidity, exhibit superior performance compared to univariate models in some
                                  other studies as well20,21.
                                      Elahe et al.22 examined the deep learning-based models, including LSTM, Bi-LSTM, CNN-LSTM,
                                  ConvLSTM, and GRU, and considered various factors affecting the charging load, such as weather, calendar,
                                  and seasons, for charging demand prediction. Three scenarios were established to investigate the impact of
                                  external factors on enhancing forecasting accuracy. The findings indicate that incorporating these important
                                  factors into prediction models enhances forecasting accuracy in all three scenarios. Chang et al.23 predicted fast-
                                  charging station (FCS) charging power consumption with an LSTM neural network. Deep learning methods
                                  like window normalization and weight initialization were used to forecast fast-charging power demand. The
                                  forecasting model was validated using the real-world EV dataset from Jeju Island, South Korea. The forecasting
                                  results indicate that the proposed LSTM model outperforms Bi-LSTM, GRU, and RNN with RMSE of 61.63,
                                  NMAE of 5.15%, and NRMSE of 6.65%.
                                      Zhu et al.24 forecast the short-term demands for CSs. The study examined four deep learning architectures:
                                  deep neural networks, conventional RNNs, LSTMs, and GRUs, utilizing a charging load sequence from the
                                  prior 24-hour period to predict the charging load for the following hour. The findings indicate that the GRU
                                  outperformed the other three models. Mohsenimanesh et al.25 evaluated the performance of three deep learning
                                  algorithms in predicting the total load required for charging a fleet of EVs: an LSTM, a Bi-LSTM, and a GRU.
                                  The Bi-LSTM demonstrated the lowest MAE, MAPE, and RMSE, proving it to be the most effective approach for
                                  forecasting EV fleet loads. Di Persio et al.26 studied EV charging forecasting utilizing various LSTM variations,
                                  including multilayer LSTM, bi-LSTM, and sequence-to-sequence LSTMs. The findings reveal that Bi-LSTM
                                  showed better results than the other methods for the specific time series load.
                                      Vishnu et al.27 implemented three techniques—AR, SVR, and LSTM—for short-term EV demand forecasts.
                                  The ACN repository provides real-world EV charging data that is utilized. The suitability of forecasting
                                  approaches for the data and its influencing factors is assessed and determined by analyzing and finalizing the
                                  Autocorrelation function (ACF) plot. The ACF plot is utilized to evaluate the randomness of the data and the
                                  correlation matrix. The latest results on the test dataset demonstrate that the models can accurately monitor and
                                  forecast the demand for EV loads. The LSTM model significantly decreases the MAE and RMSE compared to
                                  other models.
                                      Ge et al.29 proposed a technique using an improved random forest (IRF) model optimized by harmony search
                                  (HS) to minimize prediction errors for predicting the spatial-temporal distribution of EV charging loads. They
                                  integrated the charging behavior features of various EV types and the spatial-temporal coupling between EVs
                                  and charging stations. The approach also includes a parallel computational method to enhance efficiency. Case
                                  studies showed that the IRF model outperforms traditional methods like SVM and BPNN in predicting both
                                  total and spatial-temporal distribution of EV charging loads.

                                  Transformer-based techniques
                                  Koohfar et al.28 implemented the Transformer model to forecast EV charging demand. They collected real-world
                                  EV charging load data from 25 Boulder, Colorado, public CSs, each containing type 2 connectors with a 22-kW
                                  capacity. The data included charging load and weather data from January 1, 2018, to December 28, 2021. The EV
                                  charging load is predicted for three different periods: 7 days, 30 days, and 90 days. These intervals cover both
                                  the medium and long term. The model’s performance was evaluated by comparing the RMSE and MAE against
                                  traditional and deep learning models, including ARIMA, SARIMA, RNN, and LSTM. Based on the findings, the
                                  Transformer model outperformed the time series and machine learning models regarding long-term prediction
                                  performance.
                                     Xing et al.30 introduced a forecasting model incorporating actual traffic data and human decision-making
                                  behavior using Didi ride-hailing data. The model uses Regret Theory to simulate the decision-making process
                                  of EV owners, predicting fast-charging demand in urban areas. Luo et al.31 proposed an Attribute-Augmented
                                  Spatiotemporal Graph Informer (AST-GIN) by combining the GCN and Informer layers to improve prediction
                                  accuracy and interpretability and extract relevant transportation data’s external and internal spatiotemporal
                                  dependence. The training attribute-augmented encoder modelled external influences as dynamic attributes. The
                                  AST-GIN model was evaluated using Dundee City data, and the experimental findings revealed that our model
                                  was more successful than other baselines in addressing external factors over various periods.
                                     Bae and Kwasinski32 proposed a mathematical model for predicting EV charging demand at highway CSs,
                                  including fluid dynamic traffic models with M/M/s queueing theory. The model captures the spatial and temporal
                                  dynamics of charging demand. Paevere et al.33 presented a methodology for spatial-temporal modeling of EV
                                  charging demand and its impact on peak household electrical load. The model is applied to Victoria, Australia,
                                  considering various EV uptake and charging behaviors. Zhang et al.34 proposed a forecasting framework using

                                  Methodology
                                  EV charging trends and demand tend to differ at different locations in a city. The model’s performance can be
                                  evaluated based on multiple charging locations, considering the different charging trends and demand, with
                                  limited data. The temporal features based on the calendar and demand factors of the charging locations can be
                                  included in the Transformer model for accurate demand forecasting. An enhanced Transformer-based technique
                                  has been implemented to address the challenge of short-term dependencies in time series forecasting. In this
                                  research, we designed a Context-Aware Temporal Transformer (CAT-Former) model, which uses temporal and
                                  contextual features to predict EV charging demand using the EV historical data and Weather information of
                                  Boulder City. The charging location is used as a contextual feature. However, temporal features include hour, day
                                  of the week, day of the month, day type, and session duration. The external factors include weather data such as
                                  temperature, wind speed, and precipitation.
                                      In this research, a unique approach has been implemented to forecast the EV charging demand for the short
                                  term, including one-hour and one-day predictions. The performance of the proposed CAT-Former model is
                                  evaluated using the Simple and Hybrid Transformer models and other traditional baseline models. This study
                                  uses Boulder City, Colorado’s EV historical and weather data. The dataset analysis was based on various calendar
                                  features, showing the different charging trends through the data period. Furthermore, this study selects the three
                                  charging locations with the highest number of charging sessions in the Boulder City data, assuming they also
                                  have a higher charging demand. The study is focused on three charging locations within a city, and more locations
                                  could also be included for performance analysis with the proposed model. The selection of multiple locations
                                  helps evaluate the models, as the three locations have different charging trends, and the charging behaviors of
                                  the drivers may vary among these. Furthermore, the contextual and temporal features are selected and provided
                                  to the CAT-Former model. The public dataset is divided into training and test sets for the model’s training
                                  and testing. The deep learning-based base models, including LSTM, BiLSTM, CNN-LSTM, CNN-BiLSTM, the
                                  Transformer model, and a hybrid Transformer model using LSTM-based Encoder-Decoder, are compared with
                                  the CAT-Former model. The proposed model could be further extended to multi-step forecasting such as 24-
                                  hour rolling forecasting, using the input data of previous hours.
                                      As stated by Koohfar et al.28, the EV charging demand was aggregated per day for all the charging facilities to
                                  be used for day ahead predictions. The EV charging data used in our research contain hourly charging sessions
                                  that can be used for the training of the models for 1-hour predictions. However, for the 1-day predictions, the
                                  data have to be aggregated per day. Therefore, the training and evaluation of the proposed CAT-Former model,
                                  along with the baselines, are performed separately for 1-hour ahead prediction using hourly data and 1-day
                                  ahead prediction using daily aggregated data. Due to the usage of hourly and aggregated daily data, there was
                                  no need to perform a unified multi-horizon model considering the 1-hour and 1-day input data. The input
                                  provided to the baselines and the CAT-Former model includes the same data. However, the baseline models do
                                  not process this data, and the data features are not processed as contextual or temporal features, are processed
                                  in the CAT-Former model. The processing of the contextual and temporal features is performed, and then the
                                  features are used for the prediction by the Transformer model used in the proposed methodology. Figure 1
                                  shows the methodology, where green color is used specifically to highlight the steps involved for the proposed
                                  method.

                                  Proposed CAT-Former model
                                  This research designs and implements the CAT-Former model using Temporal and contextual features with a
                                  Multi-Head Attention-based Encoder and Decoder model. This subsection discusses the process of the CAT-
                                  Former model, along with the temporal and contextual features.

                                  of the data. As the research predicts the EV charging demand for the multiple charging locations, the three CSs

                                  features in the data that can be incorporated into the designed model, considering the temporal features, which
                                  can be extracted using the Charging Start Date and Time values including Hour of the day (0–23), Day of
                                  the week (Monday-Sunday), Day type (weekday/weekend), Day of the month (1–31), and Session duration

                                  a) LSTM

                                  The LSTM model is structured sequentially, with three stacked LSTM layers. The initial layer comprises 100
                                  units, processing input sequences containing 100 characteristics, succeeded by a second LSTM with 50 units and
                                  a third with 25 units, which capture long-term dependencies. Dropout layers are used following each LSTM to
                                  mitigate overfitting, whilst Batch Normalization enhances training stability. Subsequently, two Dense layers are
                                  employed, the first including 64 units for feature transformation, followed by a final single unit designated for
                                  predicting continuous values.

                                  b) BiLSTM

                                  The BiLSTM model has three Bidirectional LSTM layers, each followed by Dropout and Batch Normalization,
                                  tailored for sequential data jobs. Bidirectional LSTMs analyze sequences in both directions, encompassing past
                                  and future dependence. The initial two layers include 128 units each, while the third layer comprises 64 units,
                                  resulting in a progressive reduction of output forms. Dropout layers mitigate overfitting by randomly omitting
                                  input units, whereas Batch Normalization enhances training stability by normalizing activations. A concluding
                                  Dense layer with one unit forecasts a continuous result, signifying a regression problem.


Scientific Reports |   (2025) 15:36652                    | https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-20557-x                                            15
www.nature.com/scientificreports/


                                  c) CNN-LSTM

                                  The CNN-LSTM model combines convolutional and LSTM layers to extract spatial and temporal characteristics
                                  from sequential input. The process commences with a 1D Convolutional layer of 64 filters, which extracts local
                                  patterns and yields an output shape of (None, 28, 64). Subsequently, Batch Normalization is used for stability,
                                  followed by MaxPooling to diminish the size of the feature map. A second convolutional block employs 128 filters
                                  for enhanced feature extraction, accompanied by Batch Normalization and MaxPooling. The model proceeds to
                                  LSTM layers: the initial layer has 50 units, followed by Dropout and Batch Normalization for regularization, and
                                  a second LSTM layer with 30 units to enhance temporal pattern recognition. Dense layers, including a 64-unit
                                  layer and a single-unit output layer, forecast a continuous result, signifying a regression task.

                                  d) CNN-BiLSTM

                                  This model combines CNN and LSTM layers. The procedure starts with two Conv1D layers (64 and 128 filters)
                                  to extract local features from sequential input, followed by MaxPooling layers to reduce dimensionality. Batch
                                  normalization improves training stability, whereas two LSTM layers (50 and 30 units) proficiently capture
                                  temporal dependencies, with the first LSTM returning sequences. Dropout layers reduce overfitting, while dense
                                  layers conclude with a single output node for regression.

                                  e) Transformer

                                  The foundation of this model architecture is a MultiHeadAttention layer, which divides the input into multiple
                                  heads. This enables the model to concentrate on distinct segments of the input sequences simultaneously. The
                                  attention mechanism calculates queries, keys, and value representations, applying scaled dot-product attention.
                                  After applying attention, the output is sent through a GlobalAveragePooling1D layer to condense the sequence.
                                  The output is flattened and sent to a dense layer for regression, forecasting a singular output value.

                                  f) Hybrid transformer

                                  The hybrid model combines Transformer and LSTM architectures. The system employs an LSTM-based encoder
                                  and decoder to record temporal dependencies, using a multi-head attention mechanism to manage long-range
                                  dependencies and context. The input sequence undergoes processing by an LSTM encoder, followed by positional
                                  encoding to preserve sequential information. Multi-head attention layers are utilized to enhance the encoded
                                  representation, and then layer normalization and dropout for regularization are used. The decoder employs an
                                  additional LSTM layer to analyze the target sequence, which is followed by positional encoding and attention
                                  layers focusing on the encoder’s output. A flattened layer forecasts the output sequence.

                                  Proposed CAT-Former model The Context-Aware Temporal Transformer model integrates temporal and con-
                                  textual features to predict EV charging demand. Temporal features are encoded using a Transformer-based ar-
                                  chitecture, including hour, day of the week, and session duration, alongside contextual information like weather
                                  conditions and station location. The CS location is provided in latitude and longitude. The weather data contains
                                  the temperature, windspeed, and precipitation for the following day. The charging location and weather data are
                                  combined before being input into the model. The model leverages multi-head self-attention within the encoder
                                  to capture dependencies across time and context. At the same time, the decoder predicts energy demand for spe-
                                  cific future intervals (e.g., one hour or one day ahead). The prediction is optimized using a mean squared error
                                  loss function, enabling accurate energy demand forecasting in EV CSs.

                                  Discussion
                                  The proposed model performance, including MAE and MSE for predicting one-hour-ahead forecasting as
                                  compared to the best-performing baseline Hybrid Transformer model, is 11.82% and 5.75% lower for the park
                                  1 location, 6.23% and 12.87% lower for Rec 1, and 4.54% and 9.28% lower for street 1 locations, respectively.
                                  Furthermore, the proposed model performance for predicting one-day-ahead forecasting as compared to the
                                  best-performing baseline Hybrid Transformer model is 7.22% and 15.08% lower for the park 1 location, 3.30%
                                  and 6.40% higher for Rec 1, and 4.23% and 9.09% lower for the street 1 location. The results show that the
                                  proposed CAT-Former model can utilize the temporal and contextual features effectively for different charging
                                  locations in the city, having different charging demands and trends. The results show that the temporal and
                                  contextual features increase the model’s efficiency in forecasting short-term charging demand. Overall, the

                                  performed the analysis based on the predictions focusing on different prediction periods. The following studies
                                  don’t highlight how the weather factor contributes to the prediction accuracy for the short-term prediction.
                                  The impact of the weather factor on medium (30 days) or long (30+) days prediction can be significant due to
                                  different seasons. This research includes the weather factor in the contextual features, along with the charging
                                  station location. However, based on the evaluation approaches used in the literature focusing on prediction
                                  horizons, this proposed research also performs the analysis for the prediction horizons, such as for 1-hour and
                                  1-day predictions.
                                      The study uses Boulder City EV charging data, and selects three charging locations with the highest number
                                  of charging sessions, considering the higher demand and different user behaviour in these selected locations. This
                                  multi-location approach enables the learning framework to understand the dynamics of how charging demand
                                  could be fluctuated over different operational conditions instead of single-site behavior. The calendar features are
                                  considered as temporal, and location and weather features are combined and considered as contextual features.
                                  Because of this, the model is able to construct generalized representations that are more representative of the
                                  dynamics of real charging conditions. Considering the real-world application, the model can be applied for
                                  predictive performance using the past charging demand data without requiring any additional data.
                                      The CAT-Former model shows high applicability in the real world in predicting short-term EV charging
                                  demand across different locations. With effective utilization of historical charging data, the model will be capable
                                  of obtaining both temporal dynamics as well as contextual distinctions present in different charging stations,
                                  e.g., Park 1, Rec 1, and Street 1, having discrete usage patterns. Compared to baseline Transformer and hybrid
                                  models, the comparative performance attained in terms of MAE and MSE of one-hour- and one-day-ahead
                                  forecasts demonstrates the capacity of the model to act based on the local demand patterns. This means that
                                  CAT-Former can be considered on real charging networks, and by utilizing historical data, we can make effective
                                  short-term forecasts that can be helpful to allocate resources better, manage stations and plan the grids across
                                  sites in the city.

                                  Conclusion
                                  This research proposes a Context-Aware Temporal Transformer (CAT-Former) model using Temporal features
                                  and a Multi-Head Attention Mechanism to predict the short-term charging demand of EVs. The charging demand
                                  and behaviour at different locations tend to differ, showing the dynamic nature of EV charging. The research uses
                                  charging data from multiple locations within a city to train and test the proposed model for demand forecasting
                                  and evaluate short-term performance. The dataset used for the implementation is obtained from Boulder City,
                                  Colorado, containing historical data from January 2018 to November 2023. The data is divided into a training
                                  set from January 2018 to June 2023 and a test set from July to November 2023.
                                      The proposed model is compared with the traditional base models, LSTM and BiLSTM, and includes hybrid
                                  models such as CNN-LSTM and CNN-BiLSTM. The proposed model is compared with the Simple and Hybrid
                                  Transformer models using LSTM-based Encoder-Decoder. The results show that the proposed CAT-Former
                                  model effectively utilizes temporal and contextual features across multiple charging locations throughout the
                                  city, indicating different charging demands and patterns. Using temporal and contextual features enhances
                                  the model’s efficacy in predicting short-term charging requirements. The proposed CAT-Former model
                                  performs comparatively better than the individual and hybrid standard baseline models for one-hour and one-
                                  day predictions in most of the predictions by achieving the lowest MSE and MAE value. The CAT-Former
                                  model performs better than the Simple Transformer model; however, it shows a competitive performance as
                                  compared to the Hybrid Transformer. Overall, the proposed model performance for EV CS demand forecasting
                                  is improved, and different charging locations and trends within a city can be accurately predicted.
                                      The proposed model performance, including MAE and MSE for predicting one-hour-ahead forecasting as
                                  compared to the best-performing baseline Hybrid Transformer model, is 11.82% and 5.75% lower for the park
                                  1 location, 6.23% and 12.87% lower for Rec 1, and 4.54% and 9.28% lower for the street 1 location, respectively.
                                  Furthermore, the proposed model performance for predicting one-day-ahead forecasting as compared to the
                                  best-performing baseline Hybrid Transformer model is 7.22% and 15.08% lower for the park 1 location, 3.30%
                                  and 6.40% higher for Rec 1, and 4.23% and 9.09% lower for the street 1 location. The results for 1 h ahead and
                                  1 day ahead forecasting show that the CAT-Former model performs comparatively better than the baselines.
                                  The proposed CAT-Former model can effectively make short-term predictions using temporal and contextual
                                  features.
                                      In the future, more recent data from other cities and external factors like road traffic and networks can be
                                  included to observe their impact on the charging demand at various charging locations in a city.
